# The Government Deduction Refund is netted against Deferred Deductions first

Before the system, Government Deductions were a flat weekly amount, so months with five Pay Weeks withheld more than the shop remitted for them and four-week months slightly less. The system now splits each month's share over its own Pay Weeks, and a one-time Government Deduction Refund corrects the past from January to the end of November 2026: for each staff member, what they were charged, whether taken or deferred, less their true employee share for the months the shop remitted. 2025 is closed and not corrected: its skipped weeks were already taken from the 2025 Year-end Bonus (owner's decision, 2026-11-12). Someone who comes out under their true share gets nothing and owes nothing new; the shop absorbs it. The opening Deferred Deductions at Go-live were priced at the same flat amount, so they are over-stated by the same mistake. The refund therefore first reduces what the staff member still owes, and only the remainder is paid in cash on one Payroll, as its own line broken down by agency (owner's decision, 2026-11-12). It is applied in early December, before the Year-end Bonus takes Deferred Deductions, so the bonus sees the corrected balance. We rejected paying the whole refund in cash while leaving the owed balance as it stood, which would pay back money the staff member still owes, and refunding only the five-week months' excess, which ignores the four-week months that withheld too little. A balance that falls with no cash paid out is this netting, not an error.
